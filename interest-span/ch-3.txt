Sometimes, what someone enjoys isn't necessarily the relationship itself.

It's how the relationship makes them feel.

Think about it.

Someone messages you whenever they're bored. They enjoy your attention, your compliments and the feeling of being wanted.

They like knowing that someone is interested in them.

But when the connection starts requiring consistency, emotional availability or genuine effort, their enthusiasm begins to disappear.

This doesn't mean everyone who enjoys attention is intentionally manipulating someone.

Human relationships are complicated, and people can have mixed motivations without fully understanding them.

However, there's a difference between enjoying someone's presence and being genuinely invested in their well-being.

One is largely about what you receive.

The other also involves what you're willing to contribute.

And sometimes, when the attention is no longer new or exciting, the person simply stops putting in the same effort.
