Now let's look at the other side of the experience.

Why does someone suddenly losing interest sometimes affect us so deeply?

Why can a person we haven't known for very long occupy so much space in our minds?

The answer has something to do with social rejection.

Human beings are social creatures.

We naturally seek connection, acceptance and a sense of belonging.

Psychologist Kipling Williams examined this extensively in his research on ostracism, which refers to being ignored or excluded by others.

His research review explains that even brief experiences of exclusion can threaten fundamental psychological needs.

These include belonging, self-esteem, control and the feeling that our existence matters to other people.

This helps explain why being ignored can feel so personal.

When someone suddenly withdraws, you're not just losing their attention.

You're also losing the sense of certainty you had about the connection.

And because there's no explanation, your mind is left trying to fill in the missing information.

You replay old conversations.

You examine messages.

You search for small changes in their behaviour.

You start wondering whether one particular thing you said somehow ruined everything.

The absence of an explanation creates room for endless interpretations.

And sometimes, the uncertainty becomes more exhausting than the rejection itself.
